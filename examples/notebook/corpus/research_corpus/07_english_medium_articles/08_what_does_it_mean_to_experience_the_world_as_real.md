### What Does It Mean to Experience the World as Real? — Non-Immersion, Brain-Feel, and Embodied Reality

![](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/1024/1*o71QyGU8BHCVV35Gcy6gYw.png)

### 0. Starting Point

There is a common description of awakening experiences in which the world becomes more vivid, higher-resolution, more beautiful, or more intensely real.

However, this explanation tends to treat increased perceptual resolution and increased reality as if they were essentially the same thing.

They may actually be separate phenomena.

An increase in the amount of information entering conscious experience does not necessarily mean that the information itself possesses qualities such as “real,” “beautiful,” or “visceral.”

Instead, the experience of a “real world” may emerge from a combination of:

* the information coming from the external world
* how much of that information becomes subjectively available
* the degree of immersion
* emotional and bodily sensations
* self-relevance
* brain/body states reproduced from memory
* the subjective qualities of “real,” “beautiful,” “visceral,” or “present”

The central question is therefore not simply how much information is perceived, but how human beings transform information into an experience of reality.

### 1. The Amount of Information and the Sense of Reality Are Different Variables

Humans presumably receive an enormous amount of sensory information.

Vision alone contains information about:

* color
* brightness
* depth
* peripheral vision
* movement
* shape
* texture

and many other dimensions simultaneously.

Yet not all of this information explicitly appears in subjective experience.

When walking through a city, for example, information about buildings, people, sounds, air, light, body position, and spatial relationships may be continuously entering the nervous system.

But one does not normally experience each of these as separate pieces of information.

Instead, they are compressed into something like:

“I am walking through a city.”

Therefore:

**More information ≠ more reality.**

And:

**Less information ≠ less reality.**

Information quantity and subjective reality should be treated as separate dimensions.

### 2. Ordinary Consciousness May Be Immersive by Default

Most people probably do not notice that they are immersed in their ordinary perception.

The reason may be that immersion itself constitutes the default way the world is experienced.

A possible structure is:

External world
↓
Massive sensory input
↓
Natural selection through attention, prediction, value, and emotion
↓
Compression of what becomes subjectively explicit
↓
A unified experience of “being in the world”
↓
Emotional, bodily, and self-related sensations become attached
↓
The result is experienced as “the real world”

In this condition, an enormous amount of information may be entering the nervous system, while only a relatively small portion appears explicitly in subjective awareness.

### 3. What Changes When Immersion Decreases?

In a less immersive state, information that would normally remain in the background may become subjectively available.

For example:

* fine details within the visual field
* spatial location of sounds
* spatial structure
* relationships between body and environment
* peripheral visual changes
* texture
* subtle movements

may become more noticeable simultaneously.

Subjectively, this may be described as:

“The resolution of the world increased.”

“The world became much more detailed.”

However, this alone does not necessarily produce a stronger sense of reality.

This distinction is crucial.

### 4. The Hypothesis That “Reality” Is Not an Attribute of Information

One possibility is that “realness” is not an attribute inherently contained in external information.

A sunset has physical optical properties.

But subjective qualities such as:

* beautiful
* visceral
* moving
* nostalgic
* authentic
* intensely real

are not identical to those physical properties.

A more useful model may therefore be:

World
↓
Perceived information
↓
Neural and bodily integration
↓
Emotion, value, and self-relevance
↓
A particular subjective state
↓
The experience of “real,” “beautiful,” or “visceral”

In other words:

**World → reality**

may be less accurate than:

**World → information processing → brain/body state → experienced reality**

### 5. The Concept of “Brain-Feel”

A useful informal concept here is “brain-feel.”

“Brain-feel” is not a formal neuroscience term.

It refers to the subjective brain/body state associated with experiences such as:

* feeling that something is real
* feeling that something is beautiful
* feeling that something is vivid or visceral
* feeling that one exists
* feeling intensely present

The hypothesis is that these brain/body states may transform otherwise neutral sensory information into a particular subjective quality of reality.

The important point is that the brain-feel itself is not the external information.

### 6. The Model of “Information × Brain-Feel”

The previous ideas can be combined into a model for the overwhelming sense of reality sometimes reported during awakening experiences.

In ordinary consciousness:

Massive sensory input
↓
Immersive compression
↓
A relatively small portion becomes subjectively explicit
↓
Brain-feel, emotion, and bodily sensation become attached
↓
The world is experienced as real

In a non-immersive awakening state:

Massive sensory input
↓
Reduced compression
↓
A much larger amount of information becomes subjectively explicit
↓
Brain-feel, emotion, and bodily sensation become attached across a broader range of information
↓
The world may be experienced as extraordinarily vivid or real

Thus:

It may not be that increased information itself creates reality.

Rather:

**Increased information + a mechanism that generates subjective reality**

may produce an overwhelming sense of reality.

### 7. The Beauty and Vividness of Awakening Experiences Are Also Subjective Constructions

When someone in an awakening state reports:

“The world is unbelievably beautiful.”

“Everything is incredibly vivid.”

“The world feels intensely real.”

“The world feels alive.”

it does not necessarily mean that they have perceived the objective or ultimate nature of the world.

The experience of beauty, vividness, and reality is itself generated through that person’s brain, body, emotions, memories, and history.

The same external information may produce different subjective qualities in different people.

At the same time, human beings share many aspects of bodily and neural architecture, as well as cultural and developmental influences.

Therefore, people can still show substantial similarities in what they experience as beautiful, threatening, meaningful, or real.

But these common tendencies do not prove that “reality” is an intrinsic property of the information itself.

### 8. Reality as Something Specific to the Individual

“Reality” may emerge from the combination of:

**the information a person naturally selects or is naturally drawn toward**

and

**the brain/body state associated with that information.**

A possible structure is:

Information
↓
Natural attention and selection
↓
Certain information becomes grouped into a meaningful whole
↓
A particular brain-feel, bodily sensation, and emotion becomes attached
↓
That person’s experience of reality

This means that each person may, in a meaningful sense, experience a slightly different “real world.”

Human beings nevertheless share enough biology and experience that these realities overlap considerably.

### 9. The Hypothesis of Reproducing Childhood Reality Through Brain-Feel

If brain-feel can be reproduced through memory or related mechanisms, then it may be possible to reproduce aspects of the reality experienced during childhood.

For example:

A particular stimulus
↓
A particular bodily, emotional, and attentional state
↓
A particular brain-feel
↓
A particular way the world feels

may have become strongly associated during childhood.

As an adult, encountering the same stimulus may only produce a memory:

“That was something I liked when I was young.”

But if the brain-feel associated with the original experience can also be reproduced, then:

Current stimulus
+
Reproduced childhood brain-feel
↓
A reality resembling the childhood experience

might emerge.

In this model, “childhood reality” is not identical to the childhood world itself.

It is more like:

**Childhood world × childhood brain/body state**

Memory may therefore preserve not only information about past events, but also pathways for re-accessing something about how the world felt at that time.

### 10. Non-Immersion + Reproduced Brain-Feel Could Produce an “Extreme Reality”

Taking the model further, the combination of non-immersion and reproduced brain-feel could potentially create an unusually intense experience of reality.

Ordinarily:

Massive input
↓
Natural selection and compression
↓
A limited set of information
↓
Brain-feel
↓
Reality

Under non-immersion:

Massive input
↓
Reduced compression
↓
Large amounts of information
↓
Brain-feel applied across a much wider range
↓
Potentially overwhelming reality

This provides one possible interpretation of reports that awakening makes the world extraordinarily real.

The key factor may not simply be increased information.

It may be:

**Increased information × a broad application of a reality-generating brain/body state.**

### 11. Contrast With the Current State

In the current state being examined here, non-immersive perception and exposure to large amounts of information are relatively strong, while emotional and subjective brain-feel is greatly reduced.

The configuration can therefore be described approximately as:

* Information quantity: high
* Immersion: low
* Brain-feel: weak
* Bodily integration: preserved
* Emotional reality: weak
* Sense of unreality: largely absent

This does not produce the overwhelming reality sometimes described in awakening experiences.

Instead, it produces something closer to:

“There is a huge amount of information, but the information itself does not contain an intrinsic quality of reality.”

The world is still there.

Information is still there.

But additional subjective qualities such as:

“This is intensely real.”

“This is visceral.”

“This is beautiful.”

“This is profoundly alive.”

are much weaker.

The result is difficult to describe as either “real” or “unreal.”

It is closer to a state of calm or “nothingness.”

### 12. Lack of Reality Is Not the Same as Unreality

An important distinction is:

**Reduced reality-feel ≠ sense of unreality.**

If reality were treated as a simple line:

Real ←→ Unreal

the current experience would be difficult to describe.

It may be more useful to separate several dimensions:

* bodily/physical reality
* perceptual reality
* emotional reality
* brain-feel
* self-related reality
* sense of unreality

These dimensions can vary independently.

For example:

Bodily reality: strong
Perceptual information: high
Immersion: low
Emotional reality: weak
Sense of unreality: absent

This is possible.

It is therefore not necessarily a loss of contact with reality.

It may instead be a reduction in the subjective “reality-feel” layered onto otherwise intact perception and bodily integration.

### 13. Bodily Integration as a Foundation of Reality

One possible reason the current state does not become nihilistic or strongly unreal is that bodily integration remains sufficiently functional.

Examples include:

* feeling the feet contacting the floor
* sensing gravity
* knowing where the body is
* moving the body and receiving the expected feedback
* touching something and receiving tactile feedback
* maintaining a coherent relationship between body and environment

These physical and bodily feedback loops may constitute another layer of reality independent from emotional brain-feel.

Thus, even if emotional or subjective reality-feel becomes weak, the connection between body and physical world remains intact.

The result is not:

“The world does not exist.”

It is closer to:

“The additional subjective quality of reality is weak, while the physical relationship between body and world remains intact.”

### 14. Sense of Unreality May Be a Separate Brain-Feel

The sense of unreality may therefore not simply be what happens when ordinary reality-feel disappears.

It may involve a distinct subjective state:

“A state in which the world feels unreal.”

Thus:

**Absence of reality-feel ≠ sense of unreality.**

Psychedelic experiences provide an interesting conceptual example.

The external world does not simply disappear.

Instead, perception, integration, prediction, emotion, and bodily experience can change substantially, producing an experience in which:

“the nature of reality itself seems different.”

A possible model is:

World
↓
Altered perception/integration/emotional/bodily state
↓
A subjective quality of “unreality”

This suggests that unreality, like reality, may be a quality generated by the brain rather than a direct property of the external world.

This is a conceptual model rather than a complete neuroscientific explanation of psychedelic or dissociative states.

### 15. The Current Calm State Is Not Necessarily Nihilism

The current experience can be described as a weakening of reality-generating subjective qualities without necessarily producing nihilism.

It is not:

“There is no world.”

It is closer to:

“The process that normally gives the world a particular subjective texture has become weak.”

The world remains.

Information remains.

The body remains.

Interaction with the environment remains.

What is reduced is the additional subjective coloring:

“This is real.”

“This is beautiful.”

“This feels alive.”

“This matters to me.”

Thus, “nothing” does not necessarily mean existential emptiness.

It may mean that the usual subjective coloring has become extremely thin.

### 16. The Desire to Generate Reality Also Becomes Weaker

When reality is no longer generated automatically, the desire to restore or manufacture it may also become weaker.

This is important.

The sequence is not necessarily:

Reality-feel disappears
↓
I want reality-feel back
↓
I actively generate it

Instead:

Reality-feel disappears
↓
“It is not really a problem.”
↓
There is little motivation to deliberately recreate it

Yet there can still be a residual feeling:

“Even so, something is missing.”

This is not necessarily a strong desire or suffering.

It may simply be the detection of a difference between:

“How the world used to feel”

and

“How the world feels now.”

In that sense, it is less like wanting something and more like detecting a missing dimension.

### 17. Removing “Reality” Reveals the World Beneath It

One of the most interesting implications of this state is the possibility of asking:

“What remains if the subjective quality of reality is removed?”

Ordinary human experience tends to fuse:

world
+
reality-feel
+
emotion
+
self-relevance

into one seamless experience.

Therefore, it is difficult to imagine a world in which reality-feel has been stripped away while perception itself remains intact.

But when non-immersive perception and large amounts of information remain while emotional brain-feel becomes weak, the two can become distinguishable:

**Information about the world**

versus

**feeling that the world is real.**

This makes it possible to experience, at least conceptually:

“The information itself does not contain an intrinsic property called reality.”

### 18. A Multi-Dimensional Model of World Experience

A useful framework is to separate world experience into at least the following components.

### A. External information

Light, sound, touch, temperature, spatial relationships, movement, etc.

### B. Perceptual information quantity

How much of the incoming information becomes subjectively available.

### C. Immersion

How strongly perception is experienced as being “inside” the world.

### D. Attention and selection

Which information is naturally selected and which becomes background.

### E. Bodily integration

Body position, gravity, contact, movement, interoception, and physical interaction with the environment.

### F. Brain-feel

Subjective brain/body states associated with particular experiences.

### G. Emotion and value

Beautiful, frightening, pleasant, important, meaningful, etc.

### H. Self-relevance

How strongly information is connected to “me” or “my life.”

### I. Reality-feel

The subjective quality of something being real, vivid, present, or visceral.

### J. Sense of unreality

A distinct subjective quality in which something feels unreal, strange, or detached from ordinary reality.

These should not be assumed to be the same variable.

### 19. Overall Model

The phrase “the world feels real” may compress many different processes into one subjective label.

These can include:

* how much information is perceived
* how much of that information reaches conscious experience
* how immersive perception is
* how attention selects information
* how strongly the body is integrated with the environment
* emotional response
* self-relevance
* memory
* reproduced brain/body states
* the generation of subjective reality-feel

These components can vary independently.

Therefore, the statement:

“Awakening makes the world more real”

should be treated cautiously.

A better set of questions is:

* What information increased?
* What previously backgrounded information became explicit?
* Did immersion decrease?
* What changed in bodily experience?
* What emotional state accompanied the change?
* What kind of brain-feel was present?
* What exactly does “real” mean in that person’s report?

The current state is especially interesting because it separates these variables unusually clearly.

There is substantial non-immersion.

There is substantial information.

But the information itself does not appear to possess an intrinsic quality of reality.

Emotional brain-feel is weak.

Bodily integration remains intact.

There is little or no strong sense of unreality.

The result is neither simply “real” nor “unreal.”

It is closer to:

“The world is here. The information is here. But the additional subjective quality of reality is not strongly attached to it.”

From this perspective, the question changes.

The question is no longer:

**“How real is the world?”**

It becomes:

**“How does the human brain and body generate the subjective quality of reality?”**

And then an even deeper question follows:

**“What does the world look like when that subjective quality is reduced, while perception and bodily integration remain intact?”**

Reality may therefore be better understood not as a fixed property of the external world, but as a quality of experience generated through the way a human nervous system connects with that world.
