### 1. Starting Point

Recently, I have unintentionally developed something that could be described as a “short-sleeper-like” pattern.

However, this is not the result of deliberately training myself to sleep less.

I have always been able to put my body into a deeply restful state regardless of how much I had already slept. When I enter a strong body-sensation mode, I can relax and fall asleep quite easily.

At the same time, recently I have almost completely lost the subjective sensation of sleepiness, and maintaining wakefulness is no longer particularly difficult. As a result, my sleep duration has naturally become shorter.

Rather than simply interpreting this as “having less need for sleep,” it is more useful to separate:

* subjective sleepiness
* neural arousal
* bodily rest and recovery
* cognitive activity
* bodily awareness
* subjective quality of life (QOL)[https://medium.com/media/0b958727f0808c42b89d9b4068206ebf/href](https://medium.com/media/0b958727f0808c42b89d9b4068206ebf/href)

### 2. The Personal “Blood / Spirit” Model

“Blood” and “Spirit” are not being proposed here as literal physiological entities or as a direct claim about traditional medical theory.

They are personal phenomenological terms for describing two interacting directions of bodily experience.

“Blood” refers primarily to:

* the physical body
* deep musculature
* capillary-level bodily sensations
* bodily awareness
* rest and recovery
* the tendency toward physical relaxation

“Spirit” refers primarily to:

* the nervous system
* the brain
* cognition
* arousal
* conscious and intellectual activity

In actual physiology, the body and brain cannot be cleanly separated in this way. “Blood / Spirit” is therefore a phenomenological and operational model rather than a scientific classification.

The important observation is that these two directions can, to some extent, be intentionally influenced and their relative balance can be changed.

### 3. The Previous Baseline — “Blood” Dominant

Previously, my baseline tended to be relatively “Blood-dominant.”

I did relatively little to actively stimulate the “Spirit” side of the system. In particular, there were fewer things I wanted to do or actively engage with at the cognitive or neural level.

As a result:

Blood dominant
↓
The body naturally moves toward a chilled/restful state
↓
I lie down
↓
Even without feeling particularly sleepy, I eventually fall asleep

The important point is that this was not necessarily:

“I was sleepy, therefore I slept.”

It was closer to:

“I am not particularly sleepy, but there is little reason to keep stimulating the Spirit side, so if I simply lie down and let the body take over, I naturally fall asleep.”

Therefore, the fact that I previously slept for relatively long periods does not necessarily mean that I had a strong subjective sleep drive.

### 4. The Recent Change — Increasing Stimulation of the “Spirit” Side

Recently, especially through music, I have been intentionally increasing stimulation on the “Spirit” side.

Music is not being treated merely as entertainment.

My ongoing exploration of music concerns whether sound can selectively influence:

* cognitive clarity
* brain activity
* bodily sensations
* spatial perception
* bodily synchronization
* arousal
* neural activity

As a result, the previous balance of:

Blood > Spirit

can shift toward:

Blood ↔ Spirit

or, depending on the situation:

Spirit > Blood

When this happens, the body no longer continuously falls into a strongly chilled or sleep-oriented state. Wakefulness becomes easier to maintain naturally.

### 5. It Is Not “Forcing Myself to Sleep Less”

The current state is not one in which I am resisting sleep.

Rather:

“I can still sleep whenever I want, but there is now enough activity, interest, or stimulation on the Spirit side that I naturally remain awake.”

This is different from conventional “sleep restriction” or “short-sleep training.”

I am not deliberately reducing sleep as the goal.

Rather, my overall state appears to have changed, and shorter sleep duration has emerged as a consequence.

### 6. Subjective Sleepiness and Physiological Sleep Need Are Different

This is one of the most important distinctions.

The absence of subjective sleepiness does not necessarily mean that the body has no physiological need for sleep.

At least five variables should be distinguished:

1. Whether sleepiness is subjectively experienced
2. Whether wakefulness can be maintained
3. Whether cognitive performance can be maintained
4. Whether the body feels physically comfortable
5. Whether physiological recovery from sleep is sufficient

At present, several things are clear from my own experience:

* physiological sleepiness is almost absent
* maintaining wakefulness is not difficult
* familiar cognitive activities remain relatively stable
* bodily homeostasis does not feel significantly disrupted
* subjective QOL is high

However, it is not yet possible to conclude that my actual physiological requirement for sleep has permanently decreased.

### 7. Sleepiness Has Been “Conditioned” or Trained Down

In my own experience, the subjective sensation of sleepiness has been progressively weakened through long-term intentional conditioning.

As a result, the conventional sequence:

“I become sleepy → therefore I sleep”

has become very weak.

The current configuration may therefore be:

The body retains the ability to sleep
+
The subjective signal of sleepiness is almost absent
+
Stimulation of the Spirit side helps maintain wakefulness

This does not mean that the disappearance of sleepiness proves that sleep is unnecessary.

It means that the subjective signal directing me toward sleep has become largely separated from the underlying physiological processes involved in sleep and recovery.

### 8. Familiar Cognition vs. Unfamiliar Cognition

There is also an important difference between familiar and unfamiliar cognitive activity.

For familiar activities, the relevant models of the environment, body, and behavior are already established. Therefore, relatively little cognitive processing is required.

For unfamiliar things, I do not automatically allocate large amounts of cognitive resources unless they become necessary.

This creates something like:

Familiar activity
→ relatively little cognitive resource required
→ wakefulness is easy to maintain

Unfamiliar activity
→ resources are allocated only when necessary
→ Spirit-side activity increases
→ bodily awareness may also become more noticeable

This is relevant because the current reduction in sleepiness may not simply be a matter of “having more energy.” It may also involve how cognitive resources are allocated.

### 9. Body-Sensation Mode and the Parasympathetic-Like Feeling

When I reduce my sleep duration compared with my usual habits, I sometimes notice a somewhat stronger “parasympathetic-like” bodily feeling.

This does not necessarily mean that I am sleepy.

I can remain awake and cognitively functional while simultaneously sensing that the body itself is somewhat inclined toward rest.

This should therefore be distinguished from ordinary sleepiness.

It is also possible that this feeling simply reflects the fact that my body has not yet adapted to the shorter sleep duration.

If shorter sleep and the current Blood/Spirit balance became habitual, this bodily tendency might diminish.

That remains a hypothesis rather than an established conclusion.

### 10. The Importance of the Physical Baseline

Another important feature is that increasing Spirit-side stimulation does not currently seem to produce the level of muscular tension, stiffness, or exhaustion that it might otherwise cause.

Long-term physical work has improved my baseline bodily condition, including:

* deep musculature
* bodily awareness
* posture
* breathing
* nervous-system regulation
* internal muscular tension

As a result:

Spirit stimulation increases
↓
Spirit-side activity rises
↓
The body becomes less strongly rest-dominant
↓
Yet physical homeostasis remains relatively stable

One possible interpretation is that a sufficiently strong physical baseline has developed to support greater neural activity without immediately producing bodily breakdown.

### 11. “Both Blood and Spirit Are Full”

My current subjective assessment is that both Blood and Spirit are relatively well supplied.

On the Spirit side:

* I am alert
* cognition is available
* music can strongly affect my state
* I can engage in things I want to do
* subjective QOL is high

On the Blood side:

* bodily sensations remain accessible
* I can easily move into a deeply restful state
* chronic stiffness or strong fatigue is not dominant
* physical comfort remains relatively good

Therefore, subjective QOL is clearly high.

However:

“Both Blood and Spirit feel full”

does not necessarily mean:

“Every physiological process inside the body is optimal.”

There remains an unknown region that subjective experience cannot directly observe.

### 12. Subjective QOL and Physiological Health Are Separate Axes

This distinction is essential.

A person may have insomnia or frequently complain of being tired while nevertheless remaining active and living a long life.

Conversely, a person may:

* feel energetic
* be highly active
* experience little sleepiness
* have a high subjective QOL

while still having physiological risks that do not appear clearly in subjective experience.

Therefore:

High subjective QOL
≠
Perfect physiological health

Likewise:

Short sleep
≠
Automatically unhealthy

Subjective experience and physiological health need to be treated as separate dimensions.

### 13. “Fatigue” Also Needs to Be Decomposed

When discussing short sleep, simply saying “I am tired” is not sufficiently informative.

Different forms of fatigue should be distinguished:

* sleepiness
* cognitive fatigue
* social fatigue
* muscular fatigue
* autonomic exhaustion
* emotional fatigue
* inadequate physical recovery
* reduced motivation
* fatigue caused simply by prolonged activity

This matters because an apparent relationship between short sleep and fatigue can easily be confounded by other demands placed on the person while awake.

### 14. The Problem with Interpreting Daisuke Hori’s 30-Minute Sleep Experiment

Daisuke Hori has publicly demonstrated a 30-minute-sleep lifestyle for a week through livestreaming.

This can be an interesting case when thinking about extreme short sleep.

However, even if someone appears tired or reports fatigue during such an experiment, it does not automatically follow that:

“30-minute sleep caused the fatigue.”

The experiment also contains:

* long livestream sessions
* continuous responses to chat comments
* conversation
* decision-making
* social interaction
* constant exposure to an audience
* psychological pressure created by the experiment itself

In particular, continuously responding to livestream comments is not equivalent to simply being awake.

It requires sustained cognitive and social processing.

In the Blood/Spirit framework, this may represent a substantial load on the Spirit side.

Therefore, the fatigue caused by reduced sleep must be separated from fatigue caused by prolonged cognitive and social activity.

### 15. What Should Actually Be Compared?

When investigating short sleep, sleep duration alone is insufficient.

The relevant variables include:

* sleep duration
* total waking time
* the quality of waking activity
* cognitive load
* social load
* physical load

For example:

A: 8 hours of sleep + low-load daily life

B: 4 hours of sleep + low-load daily life

C: 4 hours of sleep + high-load work

D: 30 minutes of sleep + low-stimulation environment

E: 30 minutes of sleep + prolonged livestreaming and social interaction

These are not equivalent conditions.

The results from E cannot simply be generalized to D.

### 16. What “Short Sleeper” Means in This Model

The term “short sleeper” is too broad to fully describe the current phenomenon.

At least five different components should be distinguished:

1. Short sleep duration
2. Low subjective sleepiness
3. Ability to maintain wakefulness
4. Ability to maintain cognitive function
5. Reduced physiological need for sleep

At present, my experience strongly supports some elements of 1–4, especially low sleepiness and easy maintenance of wakefulness.

Element 5 remains unknown.

Therefore, the most cautious description is not:

“My physiological need for sleep has definitely decreased.”

It is:

“My subjective sleepiness has become almost absent, and wakefulness can be maintained naturally, so my sleep duration has become shorter.”

### 17. A Lifestyle Based on QOL

The fundamental principle behind this lifestyle is not:

“Maximize health metrics.”

It is:

“Maximize actual quality of life.”

Therefore, a conventional health practice is not automatically adopted simply because it is described as “healthy.”

The relevant question is:

Does this actually improve my:

* quality of life
* ability to act
* bodily comfort
* cognitive state
* sense of well-being
* freedom?

If a modern health practice appears not to contribute to actual QOL, I do not necessarily adopt it merely because it is conventionally recommended.

This is not the same as rejecting modern medicine.

It means that actual effects on my lived condition are given priority over authority or general rules.

### 18. But Risks That Do Not Appear in QOL Are a Separate Issue

There is an important limitation to this approach.

Maximizing subjective QOL and maximizing long-term physiological safety are not necessarily identical goals.

Some physiological variables may have little immediate effect on subjective QOL.

For example, a person may feel comfortable while long-term changes are occurring in areas such as:

* metabolism
* vascular function
* endocrine regulation
* immune function
* tissue repair

These processes may not be directly visible through subjective experience.

Therefore:

“My QOL is high, so I must be completely healthy”

cannot be concluded.

At the same time:

“My lifestyle differs from conventional health advice, so I must be unhealthy”

cannot be concluded either.

The unknown should remain unknown.

### 19. The Most Neutral Current Model

The current hypothesis can be summarized as follows:

Physical baseline is relatively strong
↓
There is sufficient reserve on the Blood side
↓
When Spirit stimulation is low, Blood tends to dominate
↓
The body naturally moves toward relaxation
↓
I can fall asleep even without subjective sleepiness

Then:

Music and other forms of Spirit stimulation increase
↓
Spirit-side activity rises
↓
Blood becomes less strongly rest-dominant
↓
Homeostasis is maintained within the body’s current capacity
↓
Wakefulness naturally lasts longer
↓
Sleep duration becomes shorter as a consequence

At the same time, subjective sleepiness itself has been strongly reduced through long-term conditioning.

Therefore, the hypothesis is not:

“Short sleep makes me healthy.”

It is closer to:

“A change in the Blood/Spirit balance has allowed me to maintain high subjective QOL and wakefulness with less sleep.”

### 20. What Should Be Observed Going Forward?

If this hypothesis is to be examined seriously, sleepiness alone is not enough.

Useful variables include:

* sleep duration
* bodily sensations after waking
* cognitive performance
* differences between familiar and unfamiliar tasks
* changes in arousal caused by music
* transition into body-sensation mode
* parasympathetic-like bodily sensations
* muscular or joint discomfort
* recovery after exercise
* appetite
* mood
* spontaneous activity levels
* long-term health markers

The particularly important question is not:

“Am I sleepy?”

but:

“What happens to physical recovery and cognitive function when this short-sleep state is maintained over time?”

### 21. The Core of the Hypothesis

This is not an argument that short sleep is inherently healthy.

The central idea is:

**Sleep, sleepiness, arousal, cognition, physical recovery, and QOL should not be treated as a single variable.**

Human subjective experience provides powerful signals such as:

“I am sleepy.”
“I am tired.”
“I feel good.”
“I feel healthy.”

But these signals do not display every physiological process occurring inside the body.

At the same time, physiological measurements alone cannot fully describe the quality of a person’s lived experience.

The personal Blood/Spirit model therefore provides a way to observe:

* how full or active the Spirit side is
* how full or stable the Blood side is
* how the two are balanced
* how that balance affects actual QOL
* what physiological processes may remain invisible to subjective experience

From this perspective, short sleep is not merely a question of sleep duration.

It becomes a broader question of:

**How does a human being distribute resources between neural activity, bodily regulation, wakefulness, rest, and recovery?**

### 22. Provisional Conclusion

At present, both Blood and Spirit appear relatively strong in my subjective experience, and subjective QOL is very high.

Sleepiness is almost absent, and maintaining wakefulness is not particularly difficult.

At the same time, the body can still transition into deep rest and sleep whenever desired.

Therefore, the current state may be better described as:

“I have maintained the ability to sleep whenever necessary, while increased Spirit-side activity has naturally extended my waking time.”

This is different from simply losing the ability to sleep.

However, high subjective QOL, low sleepiness, and preserved activity do not by themselves prove that physiological sleep requirements have decreased.

Likewise, other people’s short-sleep experiments cannot be interpreted purely in terms of sleep duration without separating cognitive, social, physical, and psychological loads during waking hours.

The most neutral position is therefore:

**Do not assume that short sleep is either inherently healthy or inherently harmful. Instead, treat Spirit, Blood, subjective QOL, arousal, sleepiness, and physiological recovery as partially independent variables and observe how they interact.**
